Privacy notice

Where MPs Stand sets no cookies and measures nothing unless you agree, never shows advertising, and needs no account. This notice explains the little it does store, and why.

Last updated 22 November 2026 (draft, for when measurement is turned on).

Data controller

Where MPs Stand is a Where They Stand project. Where They Stand, a charitable incorporated organisation registered in England and Wales (charity number NNNNNNN), is the data controller.

Contact: admin@wherempsstand.com, or admin@wheretheystand.uk for the charity.

Information stored

Your questions and the briefings written for them
Each briefing is saved with its question so that it can be shared at its own address, so that the same question can be answered again without paying for a new briefing, and so that complaints about a briefing can be checked against exactly what was shown. Saved briefings are not listed anywhere and are not linked to you. Please do not put personal details in a question. For each briefing the site also notes how long each step took, what it cost and what the automatic checks found, so that slow or poorly checked briefings can be found and fixed. These notes hold nothing that identifies you; they are kept for 13 months, and the question of a briefing that could not be written is removed from them after 90 days.
A daily visitor code in place of your IP address
To apply the daily limit on new briefings, show the occasional reminder about donations, prevent abuse and count visitors, your IP address is turned into a scrambled code. If you go through to the donation page from a reminder, that code is noted so you are not reminded again that day. The secret used to make the code changes every day and the old one is deleted, so a code cannot be linked to the next day's or turned back into an address. Your IP address itself is not stored. The code is kept with what each of your briefings cost for that day only; after the day, that record keeps the day and the cost, and no code and no time of day.
Postcodes
If you look up your MP by postcode, the postcode is sent to Parliament's constituency look-up to find your seat. The answer is kept in the server's memory for up to a day, so the same postcode can be answered again quickly; it is never written down or linked to you.
Visit counts
Pages viewed and the website that linked to the page are counted per day, as is how many visitors asked a question, asked a follow-up or opened a source. Only daily totals are kept.
Feedback and reports
If you answer “Was this briefing useful?”, the answer is saved with the question. If you report a problem, your message is saved, with your email address if you choose to give one. The email address is used only to reply to you and is deleted when the report is dealt with. A short public note may be added to the corrections page; it never includes your name or email address.
Statements under the right of reply
A member of Parliament or a devolved parliament, a peer or an organisation (or someone acting for them) can add a short statement beside what the site says about them (see If you're named). The statement is published with their name and role, as they wrote it. The email address used to confirm who they are, and to let them update or withdraw it, is never shown; it is kept while the statement is waiting or shown, and deleted three months after it is withdrawn, declined or removed. Statements are never sent to Anthropic or used to write briefings. Backups of the site's database are kept for up to two months, so a deleted address can remain in a backup until then.
Accounts
You need no account to read the site or ask a question. Accounts are only for the people who run it (and for an invite-only mode, which is off). An account holds an email address, a scrambled form of its password and its sign-in sessions. What each question asked from an account costs is noted with the account for that day only, for the daily limits. After the day, the site keeps, for each account, one line a calendar month: how many questions it asked, what they cost to the cent, and how many visitor codes it was seen with. Nothing it keeps then says which account, or which visitor, a briefing came from. That cannot hide an account whose questions were the only ones, or nearly the only ones, asked in a month, or a question that cost unlike any other. If the site were ever run invite-only, so that everyone who asks has signed in, those monthly lines could tie some briefings to the accounts that asked. A nightly backup holds the hours of its own night as they were.
Settings on your device
Your choices in Settings (such as briefing length), and your choice about measurement (below), are kept in your own browser, not sent to us. You can clear them by clearing this site's data in your browser.

Measuring visits for Google's advertising grant

Where They Stand advertises its sites on Google Search with Google Ad Grants, free advertising that Google gives charities. To keep the grant, it has to show Google that the ads lead people to use the sites. So, only if you agree, this site uses the Google tag to tell Google when you finish reading a briefing, open a source, download a briefing (in Word, or by printing it or saving it as a PDF), follow the Donate link or send a report or a statement. That is all it tells Google about: no list of the pages you read, and never your questions, the briefings written for them or anything you type.

Nothing is loaded from Google until you choose. A box at the top of the page asks you, with “Accept” and “Reject”. If you reject, or don't choose, nothing is loaded from Google and no cookie is set. If you accept, your browser loads the Google tag from Google, which sets cookies whose names start “_gcl_” on this site, and sends Google your IP address, details of your browser, the address of the page and the action. Google uses them to count how many visits from its ads led to that action. Of the page's address, and of the address you came from, only the path is sent, never anything after a “?” or a “#”, so a question in a page's address (as in a link to the home page with a question filled in) is never sent to Google.

Google receives this information as an independent controller, for its own purposes, under the Google Ads Controller-Controller Data Protection Terms. How it uses it is set out in Google's privacy policy and on its Business Data Responsibility page. Google says its “_gcl_” cookies last for 90 days (how Google uses cookies), and explains how long it keeps what it collects in how Google retains data.

The legal basis is your consent. We keep only your choice, and the day you made it, in your own browser (in its local storage, not in a cookie), and we never receive it. After a year you are asked again.

To change your mind, use “Measurement choices” at the foot of any page. Choosing “Reject” after accepting deletes Google's cookies on this site and reloads the page with nothing from Google. Clearing this site's data in your browser also removes them, and your choice.

No ads are ever shown on this site or on the charity's other sites, and nothing on them is personalised. The Google tag is told not to use your visit to personalise ads.

Information about members of Parliament

Briefings describe MPs and peers using public information about their parliamentary role: party, constituency, offices held, and, when a question asks about it, gender as recorded by Parliament and age on the date they spoke, calculated from the date of birth published on Wikidata. This is processed in the public interest of reporting on Parliament. A member who believes their information is wrong can ask for it to be corrected, using the contact details above.

Party groups and caucuses

The site shows which party groups and caucuses members belong to (the Socialist Campaign Group, the Labour Growth Group, the Conservative Environment Network and the like). This says something about their political opinions, which the law treats as a special category of information. Connections come only from members' own statements in Parliament, letters and statements they signed, and groups' own published lists, and each is shown with its source; never from press estimates. These were made public by the members themselves, or by the groups with their members' agreement, and are shown for the public interest in reporting on Parliament. The site also shows the Independent Parliamentary Standards Authority's records, published by law, of MPs' offices paying for some groups' research services, labelled as payments and never as membership. They are used for research and reporting in the public interest on how Parliament works and how public money is spent, under the exemption for research and journalism in data protection law. A member who thinks a connection is wrong can ask for it to be corrected or removed, using the contact details above. The charity's privacy notice sets out the basis in full.

Other organisations involved

  • Anthropic writes the briefings. Your question and the Hansard records found for it are sent to Anthropic's API to write each briefing. Under Anthropic's commercial terms, this is not used to train its models. See Anthropic's privacy policy.
  • Cloudflare carries visits to wherempsstand.com to the server, to shield the site from floods of automated requests. It sees each request as it passes, including your IP address, possibly outside the UK, under Cloudflare's privacy policy. Its analytics and visitor tracking are turned off for this site. If Cloudflare ever has to check that a visitor is not an automated program (during an attack, say), it may set a short-lived security cookie of its own for that purpose only.
  • Google, only if you agree to measurement: it is told when you take one of the actions above, as an independent controller (see Measuring visits for Google's advertising grant).
  • The hosting provider runs the server, in the UK, and has no access to the site's data beyond keeping it running.
  • Donations, if you make one, go through Where They Stand's Donate page to Ko-fi until the charity has a bank account, and are taken by Ko-fi under its own privacy policy. The founder receives your name, any message you choose to leave and the amount, but never your card details.

Nothing is sold or shared for marketing. Unless you agree to measurement, the site loads no third-party scripts, fonts or trackers; if you do, it loads the Google tag and nothing else.

Legal basis and how long it is kept

Information is processed on the basis of legitimate interests: running a free public service, keeping its costs within budget, preventing abuse, and correcting errors. Measurement for Google's advertising grant is on the basis of your consent, and only once you have given it. Saved briefings are kept while the site runs, unless withdrawn. Visitor codes cannot be traced to anyone after the day they are made. What an account's questions cost is kept as one line a calendar month. Report contact details are deleted when the report is closed, and the email address given with a statement three months after the statement is withdrawn, declined or removed. Server error logs are kept briefly, and requests are not logged.

People named in public records

The site republishes public records that name people: what was said in Parliament, the registers of members' interests and of all-party groups, witnesses to committees, and the Electoral Commission's register of donations, which names donors (including private individuals) as the law requires, and Companies House records of members' company roles. These are shown as published, for the public interest in seeing how Parliament works and who funds politics. Donors' and members' addresses and postcodes are left out. If you are named and think something is wrong or should not be shown, use report a problem; the official record itself can only be corrected by the body that publishes it.

Your rights

Under UK data protection law you can ask for a copy of information held about you, and ask for it to be corrected or deleted, or object to its use, and you can withdraw your consent to measurement at any time, from “Measurement choices” at the foot of any page. Because the site does not know who you are, it may need your help to find it, such as the address of a briefing or the date you sent a report. Email admin@wherempsstand.com.

If you are unhappy with the answer, you can complain to the Information Commissioner's Office.
