If you're named

If you are an MP, a peer, a member of the Scottish Parliament, the Senedd or the Northern Ireland Assembly, or a group they belong to, this site describes you. Here is how to put something right, or to have your say beside it.

We reply within 5 working days, and decide within 20 working days.

Who this is for

Members of Parliament and peers, members of the devolved parliaments where the site shows them, and organisations the site names, such as a party group or caucus. Someone acting for them, such as a member of their staff, can use it too. Everyone else is welcome to report a problem on the corrections page.

If you are not an elected representative but your name or details appear on the site (in a debate, a list of donors or a committee's witnesses, say) and you want them removed, use ask for a private person's details to be removed. These requests come first. See the privacy notice.

Three ways to raise a concern

Something is wrong

A fact, a quotation, a date, a label or a connection is incorrect, or a briefing misstates the record. We correct it, or withdraw it, as soon as we can, and say what changed and when in the corrections log.

Report something wrong

You've been characterised unfairly

The record is right, but the site's own words, labels or groupings aren't fair: a loaded word in the site's own voice, a connection without published evidence, or a grouping that suggests something the record doesn't show. We check it against the charity's impartiality policy and, if it breaks it, put it right in the same way.

Add a statement in your own words

The record is accurate and the site has followed its policy, but you see it differently. You can add a short statement beside what the site says about you. It is shown as you wrote it, marked as yours.

Write a statement

What is covered

Everything the site says about you, not only briefings. On Where MPs Stand, that includes:

  • your role and office on the day you spoke (a minister, a shadow minister, a committee chair);
  • your majority, and the band it falls in (marginal, safe and the like);
  • the party that came second in your seat;
  • connections to party groups and caucuses, including the labels for payments recorded by the Independent Parliamentary Standards Authority ("paid for the group's research service");
  • registered interests a briefing points to beside what you said (marked "Matched by AI; a link to check, not a finding of wrongdoing");
  • groupings by age and gender, first-term or re-elected, and city, town or rural seat;
  • the summary at the top of your member page;
  • briefings, and how they group and describe what you said.

The record itself is not ours: Hansard is corrected by its editors, and the registers by the bodies that keep them. We can still fix how the site reads or shows it.

Add a statement in your own words

What it can say

Up to 150 words, in your own name, about the item it sits beside: a briefing, your member page, or another page on this site that names you. Where you can, point to the record (another Hansard passage, a published statement) with a link.

What it can't contain

  • anything defamatory or abusive, or about a private individual;
  • personal information about anyone else;
  • appeals for votes, money or support;
  • anything about another person's conduct.

During an election, statements are still accepted, but not ones that campaign.

How it is shown

Beside the item it is about and on your member page, set apart from the site's own words, like this:

Statement from [your name], [the date it is published]

[Your role, for example MP for a constituency, or chief of staff to an MP]

[Your words, up to 150 words, exactly as you send them.]

[A link to the record, if you give one]

In their own words. Not checked or endorsed by Where MPs Stand.

We publish a statement as you wrote it, or decline it and say why. We never edit it, except to take out something these rules don't allow, and only with your agreement. A statement is never given to the AI that writes briefings, and is never treated as part of the record or searched with it.

How we check it's you

Before a statement is shown, we confirm it comes from you or someone acting for you, usually by writing to your parliamentary email address (ending parliament.uk, parliament.scot, senedd.wales or niassembly.gov.uk). If you write from another address, we will check with your office. A group or organisation is checked through its own published address.

Changing or withdrawing it

You can update or withdraw your statement at any time: email admin@wherempsstand.com from the address you gave. We can remove a statement that breaks these rules, and will tell you why.

Send a statement

A briefing (an address with /b/ in it), your member page, or another page on this site that names you.

Your statement is shown with your name and role once we have confirmed it's from you. Your email address is not shown. See Your information.

Thank you. We have your statement, and will write to confirm it's from you. We reply within 5 working days.

How long it takes

We reply within 5 working days, and decide within 20 working days. Anything that may be unlawful or harmful is dealt with sooner, and requests to remove a private person's details come first. Where a concern looks well founded but will take time to decide, we can hide the item meanwhile, with a note saying so.

Who decides, and how to appeal

The charity's founder decides, until the trustees appoint someone else. Every decision and its reasons are logged, and the trustees review the log at each of their meetings.

If you are unhappy with a decision, you can ask the trustees to review it: write to admin@wheretheystand.uk. If you are still unhappy, you can raise your concerns about the charity with the Charity Commission.

Letters claiming legal liability, such as defamation, go straight to the trustees. Disputed material may be taken down while they take advice.

Your information

A statement is published with your name and role, as you wrote it. The email address you give is used only to confirm who you are and to let you update or withdraw your statement. It is kept while the statement is shown, and deleted three months after the statement is withdrawn or declined. See the privacy notice.
