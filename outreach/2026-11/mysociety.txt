Hi Alex,

I hope you don't mind me writing to you directly. I couldn't find a contact for TheyWorkForYou Votes or for data licensing, so if this should go to someone else I'd be grateful if you could pass it on.

I've read your recent post about vibe-coded websites, so I'll be upfront: I'm a data guy by trade, and I've built a small family of civic sites with Claude Code, starting with Where MPs Stand (https://wherempsstand.com). I'm not after a call, funding or promotion. There's one licensing question I'd like to get right, and something I think you might find more interesting than the MPs site.

The licensing question first. Where MPs Stand shows your plain-English explanations of what voting aye (and sometimes no) meant, and your whipping notes where you have them, next to about 1,600 divisions. They come from the divisions.parquet download, each one is labelled "(TheyWorkForYou Votes)", and you're thanked on the methodology page. I couldn't find a licence for them anywhere, though, so I wanted to check you're happy for us to show them, and if so how you'd like them credited. If you'd rather we didn't use them, that's absolutely fine - we'll take them out and go back to our own neutral labels.

While I was looking into it I read your API terms, which put your own data (the lists of MPs, Lords, constituencies and so on) under CC BY-SA 2.5. I've assumed that also covers the parlparse members data we download, so every page now carries "Data service provided by TheyWorkForYou" in its footer. Our briefings are CC BY 4.0, and I've read the share-alike as applying to your member data itself rather than to a briefing that mentions who spoke. Please do correct me if I've got either of those wrong.

Honestly, the site wouldn't exist without your work. It's built on ParlParse's Hansard XML and members data, and your APPG and constituency datasets. Where you already do something well, we point people to you rather than copying it - TheyWorkForYou for alerts and MPs' pages, TheyWorkForYou Votes for voting records, and WriteToThem for contacting an MP. If anything we do turns out to be useful to your users, such as the briefings that bring together what was said on a subject across all four UK parliaments, grouped by who said it, you'd be very welcome to link the other way.

The thing I suspect you'll find more interesting is Where Councils Stand (https://wherecouncilsstand.com). It reads the minutes of full council and cabinet meetings from hundreds of UK councils, going back to 2018, and pulls out who attended, how each councillor voted in named votes (with their party at the time) and declarations of interest. As far as I can tell nobody else is doing this: councils publish their recorded votes one by one on their own sites, and nothing brings them together. It uses MapIt, so thank you for that as well. It's very much an alpha - about 1 named vote in 100 still has a councillor on the wrong side, which we say openly while we build up a properly checked test set.

On the other questions in your post: everything is free and non-commercial, and Where They Stand is applying to register as a charity. The data refreshes twice a day from your feeds, so changes of MP come through automatically, and the code is public on GitHub (https://github.com/rj-mitchell/wherempsstand). There's a corrections page and a right of reply for MPs, and anything we get wrong is corrected or withdrawn in public. The briefings are written by Claude, but only from records the site has found, and every citation and quote is checked against its source, with anything unsupported flagged to the reader. As for users, I haven't done any promotion yet. Some of the domains are still less than 30 days old, and a lot of workplace filters block new domains, so I'm waiting for that to clear first.

I've also had a chat with Tom Steinberg about it.

Thanks for TheyWorkForYou and everything around it - it's made all of this possible.
