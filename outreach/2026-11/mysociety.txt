The invitation. The site is built on your work: TheyWorkForYou's Hansard XML, the parlparse members data, and your APPG and 2025-constituencies datasets. Where you do something we don't, we send people to you rather than copy it: TheyWorkForYou for alerts and MPs' pages, TheyWorkForYou Votes for voting records, and WriteToThem for contacting representatives. If anything we do that you don't is useful to your users, you're welcome to link the other way: for example, short briefings on what was said about a subject, grouped by who said it, across the Commons, the Lords and the devolved parliaments, with every point linked to Hansard. No obligation at all.

Your post's other questions, briefly:

- Money: the site is free and non-commercial. Where They Stand is applying to register as a charity in England and Wales, and is funded by donations.
- Keeping it going: the record updates twice a day from your published data, so changes of MP at and between elections come through parlparse. The code is public (https://github.com/rj-mitchell/wherempsstand).
- Getting votes right, and complaints: we don't write our own explanations of votes. We show yours, credited, beside neutral labels. Every page has a corrections route (https://wherempsstand.com/corrections), MPs have a right of reply (https://wherempsstand.com/named), and anything wrong is corrected or withdrawn in public.
- AI: the briefings are written by AI (Anthropic's Claude) from the records found for each question, and for a vote your description is part of what the model is given. Every citation and quotation is checked against its source, any sentence no source supports is flagged to the reader, and every briefing says it was written by AI. I build the site with AI coding tools.
