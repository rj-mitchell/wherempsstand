# Decisions

The product decisions behind Where MPs Stand: what was decided, why, and when, where the date is known. Decisions
marked "family" apply to every Where They Stand site. Add a decision here, with its date, when it is made; when one
replaces another, keep the old number with a one-line pointer to the new one.

| # | Topic | Decision |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | Name and address | Where MPs Stand, at wherempsstand.com, a project of Where They Stand. The name Where They Stand and wheretheystand.uk belong to the charity (family, 31 October 2026). |
| 2 | Not a search engine | The site answers questions; it does not try to find every mention. Hansard's own search does that well, and the site sends readers to it and to the other sites on [/resources](https://wherempsstand.com/resources) for what they do better. |
| 3 | Open to everyone | No sign-in. An invite-only mode exists (`[auth] required`) and is off. |
| 4 | The record | Both Houses from the 1997 general election (the Lords from November 1999), every division (the Commons votes of 1997 to 2001 from Public Whip), and the devolved parliaments from their first sittings (October 2026). |
| 5 | Who said it | Speakers are identified by parlparse's IDs, never by name, and described as they were on the day: party, seat, role, majority and age. |
| 6 | Seat type | City, town or rural by the size of the urban area most residents live in (ONS, NRS and NISRA figures). City status does not count. [METHOD.md](METHOD.md) has the rules. |
| 7 | Independence | Independent of Parliament, every party and every campaign. The site does not use Parliament's logo or the crowned portcullis. |
| 8 | Part of a family | Every page carries the family kit: the endorsement, the footer shelf, the favicon, theme colour and link card in Commons green, and Source Serif 4 for headings (family). The open-book mark stays as the site's own. See [DESIGN.md](DESIGN.md). |
| 9 | Balance | The evidence for a briefing is chosen for balance across speaker groups (ministers, the opposition frontbench, committee chairs, backbenchers in city, town and rural seats, peers) as well as for relevance. The same plan always gives the same sources. See [METHOD.md](METHOD.md). |
| 10 | Sources only | The model is given only the chosen sources and may use no outside knowledge. If nothing relevant is found it is not called, and the site says "No records found." Every citation and quotation is checked word for word, and statements not tied to a source are counted and always shown, marked (45). |
| 11 | Neutral wording | Plain reporting verbs (said, argued, criticised, supported, opposed). Loaded words (lied, misled, U-turn, admitted, attacked and the like) appear only inside an exact, cited quotation. The site never says a member contradicted themselves; it gives what they said on each date. |
| 12 | British English | Briefings are written in British English, and a final pass converts any American spelling in the model's own words. Quotations are never changed. |
| 13 | Models | Haiku reads the question: 60 of 62 right on the test set, at a thirtieth of the cost of Opus (11 October 2026). Sonnet writes briefings: the same accuracy as Opus for half the cost (October 2026). Each is one line in `config/settings.toml` (`query_parse_model`, and `model` in `[answer]`). See [EVALUATION.md](EVALUATION.md). |
| 14 | Re-ranking | Built but off: a modest gain for several seconds' wait on every search. To be tried again once meaning-based search covers the whole record. |
| 15 | Registered interests | A speaker's registered interest is flagged beside what they said when an AI model judges it may relate, with the note "Matched by AI; a link to check, not a finding of wrongdoing." Since 22 November 2026 the flag is in amber, the family's colour for a point to check (`--wts-warn`), on the chip and in the record, so it stands apart from the record's green; and the way to the entry, "See the entry in the register", is beside the note at the top of the record rather than under the passage, after the owner could not reach it. The panel beside a briefing fits above the question box pinned under it, whose real height the app keeps in `--composer-h`, and scrolls on its own. |
| 16 | Saved briefings | Every briefing is saved at a permanent address (`/b/<id>`), with the date it was prepared and the latest record it used. Briefings are kept out of search engines unless featured. A withdrawn briefing returns 410 Gone with the reason. Embeds are an image and a link, never a frame, and no other site may frame the pages. |
| 17 | Spending caps | Daily and monthly caps for the whole site, a daily limit for each visitor and a limit on requests a minute. At a cap, briefings pause and sources keep working. A repeated question is answered from the saved copy. The figures are in [COSTS.md](COSTS.md). |
| 18 | Donations | From individuals only: none from parties, elected representatives, campaigns, lobbying firms or organisations seeking to influence policy. Any single donation over £500 is acknowledged on the About page, with the donor's agreement, or returned. A short reminder every ten new briefings a visitor writes in a day, never a paywall. The link goes to the charity's Donate page, wheretheystand.uk/donate (family, 6 November 2026). |
| 19 | No tracking | No cookies, advertising, or third-party scripts, fonts or analytics. Visitors are counted with a code made from their IP address and a secret that changes daily; the address is not stored. Cloudflare is told not to add its own analytics (`Cache-Control: no-transform`). |
| 20 | Licence | Code AGPL-3.0. Briefing text CC BY 4.0. Quotations from Hansard remain under the Open Parliament Licence. |
| 21 | No briefing for every debate | No briefing is written in advance for every debate, to hold costs down. A reader who wants one asks. |
| 22 | No labels that take a side | No labels such as "wrecking amendment". Each kind of vote has a neutral note on what voting either way usually means. |
| 23 | Speeches beside interests and donations | Not built: a timeline of a member's speeches beside their declared interests and donations would come closest to implying wrongdoing. Only to be built if a legal review clears it. |
| 24 | Topic alerts by email | Not for this site alone. To be built once for the whole family, with a consented mailing service and a change to the privacy notice (family, 6 November 2026). This site goes first, since its RSS feeds nearly provide it. |
| 25 | Raw Hansard files | Fetched over HTTPS and stored gzipped, which keeps TheyWorkForYou's corrections to old days arriving, rather than moving 1997 to 2009 to off-site storage (family, 6 November 2026). rsync, which keeps only plain files, is used only if `[sources] compress_raw_xml` is false. |
| 26 | Search vectors | Stored as 16-bit numbers: half the space for no measurable change in what is found (owner, 6 November 2026; the measurements are in [EVALUATION.md](EVALUATION.md)). The meaning-search index is rebuilt by hand when the monthly disk report says the record has grown 20% since the last rebuild (owner, 7 November 2026). |
| 27 | Recency | The recency slider reaches a half-life of 6 months, and the default is 2 years, because testers found older contributions still led at the strongest setting (owner, 12 November 2026). The 0.35 floor stays, so nothing is dropped and a much stronger old match can still lead. |
| 28 | Written questions | A written question shown as a source shows its answer under it, for the reader, but the answer is not given to the model with the question, which would cite the minister's words as the questioner's. An answer chosen in its own right is its own source (owner, 12 November 2026). |
| 29 | Who came second | The site shows the party that came second in an MP's seat at the most recent election before they spoke, with its votes and the MP's majority. It uses no polls or projections and never decides which seats are "threatened" or "targets": such wording in a question is read as who came second, and stated as that. Where a source cannot say who came second, it is "not known", never guessed (owner, 12 November 2026). See [METHOD.md](METHOD.md). |
| 30 | Party groups and caucuses | A member counts as in a party group or caucus only on published evidence, shown with its source: the group's own list, a letter or statement it published with names, or the member's own words in Parliament. IPSA records of offices paying for a group's research service are shown as that, never as membership, with no amounts, under the special purposes exemption for research and journalism (owner, 12 November 2026). No press estimates or Wikipedia. The choices on each list are recorded in `scripts/groups_from_research.py`; see [METHOD.md](METHOD.md). |
| 31 | Corrections and the right of reply | The charity's impartiality policy, section 7, built into the site (owner, 12 November 2026). Corrections cover everything the site says about a member or a group. The page for people who are named (`/named`) explains the routes, the response times (reply within 5 working days, decide within 20) and how to appeal. A named person's statement, up to 150 words, is published as written once its sender is confirmed, or declined with reasons, and is never read by search or the model. See [OPERATIONS.md](OPERATIONS.md), "Reports and statements". |
| 32 | AI briefings explained | Readers are told how briefings are written and checked, in the family's shared words (`family/GLOSSARY.md` in the charity's repository, "AI briefings"): one line under the question box on the home page, a fold-out "How the briefings are checked", and a longer section on the About and methodology pages (`#ai-briefings`). No product or company is named. Each claim was checked against the code on 13 November 2026 and must be checked again before the wording changes. |
| 33 | The shared package | The site is built on wts-core, the Where They Stand sites' shared package ([rj-mitchell/wts-core](https://github.com/rj-mitchell/wts-core)), at a fixed release. The package holds what the sites do alike; the site keeps what follows Parliament's record and every sentence a reader sees. A fix to shared code is made in the package and reaches the site as a new version, never as a local patch (family, November 2026). |
| 34 | Spending records | Once a day is over, a spending row keeps its day, counts and cost only: no visitor code, account or time of day. Each account's calls and cost are kept as one line a calendar month, and the record of each briefing keeps only the hour it was asked in. The privacy notice says so (wts-core 0.3.0, 16 November 2026). |
| 35 | Every point to check is counted | A statement whose citations all failed the check is counted and marked with the statements not tied to a source, in the note under the briefing and in the "points to check" beside its label (13 November 2026; the same on the sister sites). Since 21 November 2026 the count is in the check strip at the top of the briefing (45). |
| 36 | A reader's words are not the headline | A saved briefing's page title, link preview and card are the site's own neutral words ("Briefing from Hansard, 18 November 2026"), worked out when the page is shown, so briefings saved earlier have them too. Neither the reader's question nor the model's reading of it is used, since either can repeat an allegation under the site's name. On the page the question is shown labelled "Question asked by a reader". A featured briefing may have the owner's own title (18 November 2026). |
| 37 | Follow-ups carry only what the site wrote | A follow-up names the earlier briefings by their addresses, and the start of each is read back from the saved briefing. Answer text sent by the browser is ignored, so no one can put words into an "earlier briefing" (18 November 2026). |
| 38 | Questions that are not about Parliament | When the small model reading the question finds it plainly is not a question about Parliament's record (a request for a poem, instructions to the site), nothing is searched and no briefing is written: the reader is told "No records found." A loaded or hostile question about what members said is still answered, and a reader who edits the search has it run. Each visitor may have 10 new briefings a day, down from 40, so a few visitors cannot use up the daily cap between them (18 November 2026). |
| 39 | Interests and IPSA records stay | The owner decided to keep registered interests flagged beside speeches (15) and IPSA records of offices paying for a group's research service (30), on the special purposes exemption for research and journalism, with no further legal review. The timeline of speeches beside interests and donations (23) is still not built (19 November 2026). |
| 40 | Search engines | Search engines may fetch the free, read-only addresses each page draws its content from, so a page drawn by a search engine shows what a reader sees; briefings, searches, anything that writes, a reader's own details and the owner's pages stay closed to them (`web/public/robots.txt`). A page whose content cannot be fetched keeps the plain copy the server wrote into it, under a short notice, rather than an error alone. Every page of the record is in a sitemap (debates and early day motions a year each, former members as well as sitting ones), dated only where the record gives a true date, and can be reached by plain links from the index pages: bills by year, motions by month, former members, devolved sittings by year and every sitting week each have an archive page. Each page has one address: a member's address without their name, or with an old one, is sent on to it, and the frames the pages are drawn in are not pages at their own addresses (19 November 2026). |
| 41 | Councils on an MP's page | A sitting MP's page lists the councils whose areas overlap their seat, lower and upper tier, from the ONS lookup of wards to constituencies and councils (July 2024, Open Government Licence), kept with the code by `scripts/build_council_overlaps.py` (`data_static/geo/constituency_councils.csv`). A council overlaps a seat when any of its wards, or part of one, is in it; the county council comes first, then the others by how many of the seat's wards they hold. Each links to its page on Where Councils Stand, under that site's name and spine. A link goes only to a council that site lists (its public `/api/councils`): a copy kept with the code, fetched again by the twice-daily update, which keeps the last good copy if the site cannot be reached or its answer is not the list. Nothing is asked of another site while a page is served. Barnsley and Sheffield are linked by the codes Where Councils Stand uses for them. Former MPs and peers have no list, since the lookup is for the 2024 seats only, and strategic authorities are left out for now. The server writes the list into the page as well (decision 40). Charity review plan, C1 (21 November 2026). |
| 42 | Quoted in | A member's page lists the topic briefings (`/topics`) that quote them, newest first: "Quoted in" with each topic's title, linked to its page, and the day its current version was prepared. A topic counts when its current version cites something the member said in a citation that passed the check; earlier versions, featured briefings and readers' briefings do not count. It is made from the saved briefings' sources, with no AI, and the server writes it into the page as well. Charity review plan, C1 (21 November 2026). |
| 43 | The same question on a sister site | A briefing, live or saved, offers "Ask the same question on Where Councils Stand" when the question as read touches local services, and "Ask the same question on Where Countries Stand" when it touches foreign affairs, with each site's name and spine. It is judged from the policy areas (portfolios) in the plan the site made when it read the question, with no further AI, and replaces the earlier rule on the question's words alone. Local services: housing, planning, communities and local government (`housing_communities`) always; health, education, transport, the environment and the Treasury only when the question or the words searched for name the part councils run (social care, schools and SEND, buses and local roads, bins and recycling, council tax and business rates). Foreign affairs: the Foreign Office, international development, defence and international trade (`foreign`, `international_development`, `defence`, `trade`). Relations with the EU are not counted, since Where Countries Stand holds the UN's record. A briefing with no plan offers neither. The line is drawn by the app, so it is not in the server's copy of a briefing (`web/src/sisters.ts`). Charity review plan, C1 (21 November 2026). |
| 44 | Footer links | Every page's footer, the owner's admin page included, has the family's six links in its order: About, Methodology, Corrections, Accessibility, Privacy, Source code. "If you're named" is linked from Corrections and About, one step away, and is not a seventh footer link (family/GLOSSARY.md; charity review plan, C4, 21 November 2026). |
| 45 | Checked briefings, in the family's design | Every briefing, live or saved, shows its checking as design, in the family's direction B, "ledger" (the charity's review plan, B1 to B3, and family/BRIEFINGS.md; kit 2.1.0's `briefing.css`). The check strip "Checked against Hansard" opens it, with the three counts in words from the answer as shown (wts-core's `check_counts`), each with something to check saying "To check" and linking to the first one; it replaces the badge beside the label and the note under the text, and the full note "How this briefing was checked", with the verifier's own counts, stays at the end, linked from the strip. A quotation found word for word is set in the record's typeface on the record's highlight with "✓ word for word"; one not found says "Not found word for word". Chips replace the numbered citations: the member's name, "Commons", "Lords", the devolved parliament ("Senedd", "Scottish Parliament", "Northern Ireland Assembly") or "Committee evidence", and the date in full; an early day motion's chip names the motion, and a division's its number. A chip is named by its own words ("Source 3 Emma Reynolds, Commons, 3 November 2026"), with no separate label, so what is said to it is its name (WCAG 2.5.3). A speaker's registered interest that may relate (15) is said on the chip in words, "Registered interest". On wide screens (64rem and over) the record is beside the briefing from the start, open on the first source the text cites, following the chip the reader chooses, hovers or focuses and, as they scroll, the chip nearest the upper third of the screen, joined to it by a line; on narrower screens a chip opens the same slip in a bottom sheet. The slip is a Hansard column: the House, the date and the column, the speaker in bold with their party and seat, the debate's heading, the passage with every quoted run highlighted and the chosen one outlined, and a link to the official report; "All the details of this source" opens the source panel the site had before (the speaker's role, majority and registered interests). Text with no source is always shown, with a dotted underline, "No source" on the first and words for screen readers on each, never behind a button and never grey: the owner's answer 9 of 21 November 2026, which replaces "Show them" (10, 35). Printed, chips are their numbers and the sources are listed under the briefing; the Word download is unchanged but for the check summary at the top. Charity review plan, B1 and B3 (21 November 2026). |
| 46 | Quotations beside their citation | Every answer a reader is sent, a new one, one played from the cache and a saved or topic briefing at its address, passes through wts-core's display rule (`quotes_beside`, in `rag/checks.py`): a quotation the verifier found word for word in the source cited straight after it is shown as that source's quotation, with its chip, and the words around it keep their mark. Only what is shown changes: the saved and cached copies keep the verifier's own answer and counts. The briefing's instructions also tell the model to attach each citation to the claim and its quotation, never to a date or other words beside them (grounding rule 2); the instructions' version changed with them, so every cached answer is written afresh. Charity review plan, B1 (21 November 2026). |
| 47 | The wait | While a briefing is prepared the page shows its stages, each drawn from the stream's own events and nothing else: the family's spines until the first event; the question as read, in large chips, on the plan event; the records found as cards in the form of a Hansard column, with their real first words, dealt into a pile beside the text (along a shelf on phones) on the sources event, with a running count and then the summary ("34 records from 31 speakers: 21 from Hansard, 7 from the devolved parliaments and 6 from committee evidence. Writing from these 34 records only. The AI may use these and nothing else."); each record marked "Cited" as the model's citation events name it; from the stream's checking event (sent once the model has finished, before the checks and the registered-interests lookup they wait for) the stage "Checking every citation and quotation" as Now, the summary saying "Written from these 34 records only." rather than "Writing", and under the text the kit's checking line, "Checking every citation and quotation against Hansard. The briefing is finished when this is done.", with a moving bar (the status region says the same words once); and on the final event the strip's counts running up through the real results while each quotation takes its highlight, then the pile settling into the record beside the briefing. Chips are not drawn while the text streams: a citation event does not say which words it belongs to, and a chip in the wrong place would say the wrong thing, so the finished text with its chips replaces the streamed text at once when the briefing ends. Nothing waits for an animation: the cards are dealt within about a second and at once when text arrives, the run-up takes at most about a second and a half, a chip pressed ends it, and with reduced motion there is none. One status region says each stage once and the counts once, in words. The text is drawn at most every 80 milliseconds, the finished paragraphs once, and the source list stays folded away until the briefing ends, so a phone keeps up: on a 390-pixel screen with the processor slowed four times, a whole briefing's stream had about a dozen long tasks (under a second in all over twenty seconds, the longest the final event's) and 95 in 100 frames within 33 milliseconds. "Sources only (no AI)" shows the first two stages, then the list. Charity review plan, B6 (21 November 2026). Kit 2.3.0 (22 November 2026), after the owner found the last seconds looked finished while the checks ran: something always moves while the site works (the five books taken down in turn before the first event, a pulsing dot beside "Now", the checking line's bar), and nothing moves once it has finished. |
| 48 | The checking on the home page | The home page shows the checking in a block of its own under the question box, in place of the grey side box: the three steps, a real sentence from the topic briefing on the cost of living (`q3k4u3vqh8`, prepared 17 November 2026, kept as a fixed copy in `web/src/__tests__/fixtures/briefings/`) with its record quotation and chip, beside the Hansard column it came from with the same words highlighted, and a link to the methodology. It is plain HTML in the page (`web/partials/proof.html`), which readers without scripts and search engines see too, and the app shows the same file. The family's line and "How the briefings are checked" (32) stay just under the question box, and "Sources only (no AI)" stays an equal choice beside it. Charity review plan, B4 (21 November 2026). Since kit 2.3.0 (22 November 2026) the block has the family's markup, two cards of one height labelled "In a briefing written by AI" and "In the record it cites", and the family's line and the fold-out are part of it, the line under its heading and the fold-out at its foot above the methodology link, rather than a line straight above a block that said the same, as the hub has them. |
| 49 | Share cards that show the checks | A saved briefing's link preview (`og:image`) is its own card, drawn once by wts-core's `briefing_card` in Commons green with the site's typefaces and mark, and kept on disk: "Briefing written by AI, checked against Hansard", the briefing's neutral title (36), the strip's three counts in words and the day it was prepared. Each topic page has its own card the same way, titled with the topic's own title. The alt text says what the card says. Charity review plan, B5 (21 November 2026). |
| 50 | Measurement for Google Ad Grants, built and switched off | The charity will apply for Google Ad Grants once it is registered, which needs the Google tag on the sites to count conversions (the charity's review plan, Part D, and its `family/MEASUREMENT.md`). It is built now and off, so the site is what it was until the owner turns it on ([OPERATIONS.md](OPERATIONS.md#measurement)). The switch is `[ads] measurement` and `tag` in `config/settings.toml`, false and empty; on the server `HANSARD_ADS_MEASUREMENT` and `HANSARD_ADS_TAG` in `deploy/.env`. It is read when the site starts, not at build time, so the pages are changed as they are sent (`src/hansard/api/measure.py`) and the built files stay as they are. Off, every page is byte for byte what it was but for the family kit 2.2.0's version and its inert `data-wts-conversion` marks. On, every page loads the kit's `measure.js`, which asks first and loads the Google tag only after "Accept"; the footer gains "Measurement choices" on a line of its own after the notices (a control, not one of the six footer links, 44); `/privacy` is `web/privacy-measurement.html`, in the glossary's "once registered" words, never served at an address of its own and refused at start-up and by `hansard check-settings` while it still says `NNNNNNN` or "(draft"; and every answer carries the front door's policy with the kit's Google hosts added (`measure-csp.json`). One switch for this site: the front door sends its own policy only when the site sends none, so this site's policy follows its own switch, and `MEASURED_SITES` is for the other sites on the server. What is measured: the end of a finished briefing coming into view (once each briefing), a source chip, source card or link to the record, the Word download and "Print or save as PDF", the Donate links (the kit's shelf, the reminder and About's), and a report or statement sent (the forms need script, so the site says so when one is sent; there is no thank-you page). Nothing else: no page views, never a question or a word of a briefing, and only the page's path, never its query (22 November 2026). |
