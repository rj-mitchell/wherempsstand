# Decisions

Product decisions for Where MPs Stand, made by the owner. They override anything else in this repository. Add new
decisions here once they are made, with the date.

This list was put together on 6 November 2026 from what the site's documents of October 2026 already recorded (the
README, `docs/EVALUATION.md`, `docs/COSTS.md`, `docs/HOSTING.md`, `config/settings.toml`, the methodology page and
the open items moved from the pre-public repository, `rj-mitchell/hansardgpt`), and from the charity's family plan
(`plans/family/PLAN.md` in `rj-mitchell/wheretheystand-charity`, section 8), whose decisions apply to every site.

## What the site is

| # | Topic | Decision |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | Name and address | **Where MPs Stand**, at wherempsstand.com. It was called Where They Stand, at wheretheystand.uk, until 31 October 2026, when that name and address passed to the charity. The repository was renamed from `wheretheystand` to `wherempsstand` on 6 November 2026 (family decision 7). |
| 2 | Not a search engine | The site answers questions; it does not try to find every mention. Hansard's own search does that well, and the site sends readers to it, and to the other sites listed on [/resources](https://wherempsstand.com/resources), for what they do better. |
| 3 | Open to everyone | No sign-in. An invite-only mode exists (`[auth] required`) and is off. |
| 4 | The record | Both Houses from the 1997 general election (the Lords from November 1999), every division including the Commons votes of 1997–2001 from Public Whip, and the devolved parliaments from their first sittings. The archive first started in 2010 and was extended in October 2026. |
| 5 | Who said it | Speakers are identified only by parlparse's IDs, never by name, and described as they were on the day: party, seat, role, majority and age. |
| 6 | Seat type | City, town or rural by the size of the urban area most residents live in (ONS, NRS and NISRA figures), not by city status. |
| 7 | Independence | Independent of Parliament, every party and every campaign. The site does not use Parliament's logo or the crowned portcullis. |
| 8 | Part of a family | A Where They Stand project, carrying the family's binding on every page: the endorsement, the footer shelf, the favicon, theme colour and link card in Commons green, and Source Serif 4 for headings (family decisions 1 and 4). The open-book mark stays as the site's own mark on the page. See [DESIGN.md](DESIGN.md). |

## Briefings

| # | Topic | Decision |
|---|---|---|
| 9 | Balance | The evidence for a briefing is chosen for balance across speaker groups (ministers, the opposition frontbench, committee chairs, backbenchers in city, town and rural seats, peers), not keyword score alone. The same plan always gives the same sources. See [METHOD.md](METHOD.md). |
| 10 | Sources only | The model is given only the chosen sources and may use no outside knowledge. If nothing relevant is found it is not called at all, and the site says "No records found." Every citation and quotation is checked word for word, and statements not tied to a source are counted and can be shown. |
| 11 | Neutral wording | Plain reporting verbs (said, argued, criticised, supported, opposed). Loaded or judgemental words (lied, misled, U-turn, admitted, attacked and the like) appear only inside an exact, cited quotation. "Claimed" only as a verb of saying. The site never says a member contradicted themselves; it gives what they said on each date. |
| 12 | British English | Briefings are written in British English, and a final pass converts any American spelling in the model's own words. Quotations are never changed. |
| 13 | Models | The smallest, cheapest model reads the question (the filters shown as chips), from 11 October 2026: 60 of 62 right in the test set, at a thirtieth of the cost of the largest. Briefings were written by the largest model until later in October 2026, and by the middle one since, for half the cost with the same accuracy. Each is one line in `config/settings.toml` (`query_parse_model`, and `model` in `[answer]`). See [EVALUATION.md](EVALUATION.md). |
| 14 | Re-ranking | Built but off: a modest gain for several seconds' wait on every search. To be tried again once meaning-based search covers the whole record. |
| 15 | Registered interests | A speaker's registered interest is flagged beside what they said when an AI model judges it may relate, with the note "Matched by AI; a link to check, not a finding of wrongdoing." |
| 16 | Shared briefings | Every briefing is saved at a permanent address (`/b/<id>`), with the date it was prepared and the latest record it used. Briefings are kept out of search engines unless featured. A withdrawn briefing returns 410 Gone with the reason. Embeds are an image and a link, not a frame, so pages can't be framed by other sites. |

## Money

| # | Topic | Decision |
|---|---|---|
| 17 | Spending caps | $10 a day and $150 a month for the whole site; 40 new briefings a visitor a day; 20 requests a minute. When a cap is reached, briefings pause and sources keep working. A repeated question is answered from the saved copy for up to a week. See [COSTS.md](COSTS.md). |
| 18 | Donations | Accepted from individuals only; none from parties, elected representatives, campaigns, lobbying firms or organisations seeking to influence policy; any single donation over £500 acknowledged on the About page, with the donor's agreement, or returned. A short reminder every ten new briefings a visitor writes in a day, never a paywall. Since 6 November 2026 the link goes to the charity's Donate page, wheretheystand.uk/donate, not straight to Ko-fi (family decision 9). |

## Readers' privacy and the licence

| # | Topic | Decision |
|---|---|---|
| 19 | No tracking | No cookies, no advertising, no third-party scripts, fonts or analytics. Visitors are counted with a code made from their IP address with a secret that changes daily; the address is not stored. Cloudflare is told not to add its own analytics (`Cache-Control: no-transform`). |
| 20 | Licence | Code AGPL-3.0. Briefing text CC BY 4.0. Quotations from Hansard remain under the Open Parliament Licence. |

## Decided against, or not yet

| # | Topic | Decision |
|---|---|---|
| 21 | A briefing for every debate | No ready-made briefing is written and stored for every debate, as for bill stages, to hold costs down. A reader who wants one asks. |
| 22 | Labels that take a side | No labels such as "wrecking amendment". Each kind of vote has a neutral note on what voting either way usually means. |
| 23 | Speeches beside interests and donations | Parked: a timeline of a member's speeches beside their declared interests and donations would come closest to implying wrongdoing. Not to be built unless a legal review clears it. |
| 24 | Topic alerts by email | Not for this site alone. Planned once for the whole family, with a consented mailing service and a change to the privacy notice (family decision 15, 6 November 2026); this site goes first, since its RSS feeds nearly provide it. |
| 25 | Raw Hansard files | Compressed in place, which keeps TheyWorkForYou's corrections to old days arriving, rather than moving 1997–2009 to off-site storage (family decision 14, 6 November 2026). Built on 6 November 2026: the sync fetches over HTTPS and stores each file gzipped; the files already on the server are compressed once with `hansard compress-raw` ([HOSTING.md](HOSTING.md), "Disk space"), which reads nothing again. rsync, which can only keep plain files, is used only if `[sources] compress_raw_xml` is set to false. |
| 26 | Search vectors | Stored as 16-bit numbers rather than 32-bit: half the space (about 6–7 GB on the server) for no measurable change in what is found, approved by the owner on 6 November 2026 (the charity's server review, R9). Embeddings are still computed in 32 bits and rounded when stored. New tables are 16-bit; the tables already on the server are converted once with `hansard vectors-f16`, which checks the new copy against the old before swapping it in ([HOSTING.md](HOSTING.md), "Disk space"; the measurements are in [EVALUATION.md](EVALUATION.md), "Retrieval only"). The passages' vectors first, the committee evidence's in the same run; the sister sites can follow. Converted on 7 November 2026 (6.4 GB freed). The new index needed 80 partitions searched rather than 20 to find as much ([EVALUATION.md](EVALUATION.md)), so `vectors-f16` now measures and saves its settings as `reindex-vectors` does. The index is not rebuilt automatically: each rebuild is recorded, and the monthly disk report says when the record has grown 20% since (the owner, 7 November 2026). |
| 27 | Recency | The recency slider reaches a half-life of 6 months (was 2 years), and the default is 2 years (was 4), from testers' feedback that older contributions still led at the strongest setting (owner, 12 November 2026). The 0.35 floor stays, so nothing is dropped and a much stronger old match can still lead; lowering it is the next step if recent contributions should lead further. Readers' saved settings keep the half-life they chose. |
| 28 | Written questions | A written question shown as a source shows its answer under it, for the reader. The answer isn't given to the model with the question, which would cite the minister's words as the questioner's; when the answer is itself chosen, it is its own source, with the question as its context (owner, 12 November 2026). |
| 29 | Who came second, not projections | Testers asked whether, say, Labour MPs in seats "threatened by Reform" think differently from other Labour MPs. The site uses no polls or projections and never decides which seats are threatened, at risk or targets. It shows the fact instead: the party that **came second** in the MP's seat at the most recent election before the day they spoke, with its votes and the MP's majority over it, beside the majority it already showed. Questions can be limited to MPs in seats where a party came second, or grouped by it. Wording such as "threatened by", "facing" or "target seats" is read as that fact, and the filter chip, the plan given to the model and the briefing state the fact ("seats where Reform UK came second at the last election before they spoke"), never a threat; no majority filter is added unless the question asks about marginal or safe seats. The Brexit Party counts as Reform UK (the same registered party, renamed in January 2021), each result naming it as it stood then. Where a source cannot say who came second (the Library's 1997–2005 file adds smaller parties together as "Other", a tie, a single candidate) it is "not known", never guessed (owner, 12 November 2026). See [METHOD.md](METHOD.md) and the methodology page. |
| 30 | Party groups and caucuses | A member counts as in a party group or caucus (the Socialist Campaign Group, the Labour Growth Group, the Conservative Environment Network and the like) only on published evidence, shown every time with its source: the group's own list, a letter or statement they signed that the group published with names, or their own words in Parliament (first person only, found by rule, precision over recall). IPSA's records of MPs' offices paying for a group's pooled research service are kept as their own kind, labelled "paid for the group's research service (IPSA records, years)" and never called membership, and shown from 12 November 2026 (owner) on the basis of the special purposes exemption for research and journalism (`[groups] show_ipsa = true`; the charity's policies/special-category-data.md, section 3), with no amounts; claims whose service and description name different groups are left out as unclear. Evidence counts for the Parliament it belongs to; a self-declaration for the whole of its Parliament. The owner's choices on the research of 12 November 2026: the Socialist Parliamentary Research Group stays separate from the Socialist Campaign Group; the SCG's 2026 pamphlet contributors are left out; Mainstream is included, as signatories; the Conservative Environment Network's undated page gives sitting MPs and peers only, with its access date, to be checked again; the party is the one on the list's date; the Common Sense Group's letter of 9 November 2020 is used; the Net Zero Scrutiny Group's letter of 2 January 2022 is used, its 20 names checked by the owner against the archived Telegraph original (archive.ph/LaeNS), and the letter of 28 June 2023 its chair coordinated is not used. No press estimates or Wikipedia. Most groups publish no lists, coverage is uneven between parties, and a group with no evidence is not offered; the site says so (owner, 12 November 2026). See [METHOD.md](METHOD.md). |
| 31 | Corrections and the right of reply | The charity's impartiality policy, section 7 (November 2026), built into the site (owner, 12 November 2026). Corrections cover everything the site says about a member or a group, not only briefings: a member's role and office on the day, their majority and its band, the party that came second in their seat, party-group connections (IPSA payment labels included), registered-interest flags, groupings by age, gender, tenure and seat type, member-page summaries and briefings. The report form offers the policy's routes by the family glossary's names ("Something is wrong", "You've been characterised unfairly"), plus a private person's details to remove, which come first; the stored values are unchanged (`correction`, `complaint`, `general`, with `takedown` added) and `?kind=` chooses one, also as `wrong` or `unfair`. A page for people who are named, "If you're named" (`/named`), linked from every footer beside "Corrections and problems" and from the corrections page, explains the routes, what is covered, how a statement is checked and shown, the response times ("We reply within 5 working days, and decide within 20 working days.", also on the corrections page), who decides and how to appeal. The third route, a statement in someone's own words of up to 150 words, is held as pending until the owner has confirmed who sent it (`hansard statements` or the admin page), published as written or declined with reasons, never edited, and shown beside the item it is about (a briefing beside the member's first source card, with a pointer under the briefing; other pages at the end of their content) and on the member's page, headed "Statement from [name], [date]" and followed by "In their own words. Not checked or endorsed by Where MPs Stand.", in the sans inside a dashed rule. Statements are kept in the site's own state database, apart from the record: the briefing pipeline, search, the index and the pages' readable text never read them (tests/test_statements.py). The email address given is deleted three months after a statement is withdrawn or declined (the policy's bracketed figures, 150 words, 5 and 20 working days and three months, are used as they stand). See [OPERATIONS.md](OPERATIONS.md), "Reports and statements". |
| 32 | Why use this rather than a chatbot? | Readers are told plainly why the site is worth using instead of a general AI chatbot, in the family's shared words (`family/GLOSSARY.md` in `rj-mitchell/wheretheystand-charity`, "Why use this rather than a chatbot?"; the charity's `wts-core` brief, phase 1b; 13 November 2026). On the home page, just under the question box: one line that is always shown, "Answers only from the official record, every point linked and checked.", and a plain fold-out (`<details>`) with five one-line points (only the record; every point linked and checked; up to date; it says when there is nothing; balanced by design) and "Briefings here are written by AI too. The difference is what the AI is given and how it is checked." It must be seen without scrolling on a phone and a desktop and must not push the question box down, so the note that the site covers more than MPs moved from above the box to under the fold-out (the box sits higher than it did), and the fold-out opens downwards. The list "How this differs from a search" at the foot of the home page keeps its first point; its other two ("Balanced by design", "Checkable") are now said in the fold-out. The About and methodology pages say the same seven points at more length (`#chatbot` on each; the About page's earlier section, "Why not just ask an AI chatbot?", is replaced). No product or company is named, and nothing is said about what others get wrong. Each claim was checked against the code on 13 November 2026 and must be checked again before it is changed. Three wordings are this site's own, because the family's would not be true here: "the Hansard and other parliamentary records" (early day motions, committee evidence and the devolved parliaments' reports are given to the model too), "any statement no source supports" (short joining sentences are not counted), and balance "across ministers, opposition and backbenchers" (party is shown, but sources are not chosen by it; decision 9). Nothing counts how often the fold-out is opened; a count, with no reader tracking, is to come with the per-briefing statistics. |
